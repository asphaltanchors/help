Installation time depends less on the anchor itself and more on the drill, the asphalt, hole cleaning, adhesive choice, and how well the work is batched. For the installation steps themselves, see the [Asphalt Anchor Installation Guide](/installation/anchor-install/).

## Drilling

Drilling is usually the biggest share of the time. With a Bosch RH745 rotary hammer, we measured:

| Hole diameter | Product | Time per hole |
| --- | --- | ---: |
| 7/8" | [[SP10]] | 0:15 |
| 1-1/8" | [[SP18]] | 0:30 |
| 1.5" | [[SP58]] | 1:45 |

A smaller drill is slower: a $150 SDS-Max rotary hammer took 2:30 for the same 1.5" hole. Difficult asphalt can double drilling time. See [Hammer Drill Size and Installation Time](/installation/hammer-drill-size-installation-time/) for the full comparison and when a bigger drill pays off.

Keep the bit sharp and clear the flutes after each hole. Packed flutes slow the hole and can jam the bit.

## Hole Cleaning

Every hole gets brushed with a brush on a drill, blown out, brushed again, and blown out again. Plan time for this on every hole; it directly affects bond strength. See [Clean the Hole](/installation/hole-cleaning/).

## Adhesive

* **[[EPX2]]** is the least expensive, but it has to be mixed, in batches of at least 4 anchors. It works best with a crew: one person mixes, one pours, and one sets anchors. Anchors can be loaded after about 2 hours at 75°F.
* **[[EPX3]]** needs no mixing and is the fastest for small jobs and one-person crews. Anchors can be loaded after about 10 minutes in hot weather and about 1 hour in cold weather.

If anchors must be loaded the same day, include cure time in the plan. See [EPX2 vs EPX3 Adhesive](/product-info/adhesive-options/) and [Weather and Temperature Installation](/installation/bad-weather/). To estimate how much adhesive you need, use the [adhesive calculator](https://asphaltanchors.com/pages/adhesive-calculator).

## Batching

Small jobs feel slower per anchor because setup, mixing, and cleanup are spread across fewer anchors. On larger jobs, batch the work:

1. Mark all locations.
2. Drill all holes.
3. Brush and blow out all holes.
4. Place adhesive.
5. Insert anchors.
6. Clean excess adhesive.

Batching reduces tool changes and makes adhesive handling more predictable. It also helps avoid rushing hole cleaning. For jobs with many anchors, such as traffic calming, see [Speed Bump Installation](/installation/speed-bumps/).

## Total Installed Cost

The cheapest tool or the fastest-looking shortcut does not always give the lowest total cost. A faster drill, the correct bit, clean holes, and the right adhesive reduce labor without giving up anchor strength. A failed anchor and a callback cost far more than the time saved by skipping a step.

For projects exposed to road salt, coastal weather, or chemicals, anchor material also affects lifetime cost. See [Zinc Plated vs Stainless Steel Anchors](/product-info/zinc-vs-stainless/).

## Planning Checklist

Before estimating a job, confirm:

* Anchor model and hole size
* Number of anchors
* Asphalt thickness and condition
* Weather and whether holes will be wet
* Drill size and bit condition
* Adhesive
* Whether the anchors need to be loaded the same day

The best estimate comes from timing a small test batch with the same crew, tools, anchor, and asphalt as the full job.
