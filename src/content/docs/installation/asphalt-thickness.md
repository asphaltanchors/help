## Why the Hole Is Deeper Than the Asphalt

**You do not need 12" of asphalt.** Our anchors call for holes 6" to 12" deep, while most residential asphalt is only about 3" thick. Commercial pavement can be much thicker, sometimes 6" or more, but the hole usually still goes deeper than the asphalt. That difference is by design. Pavement is built in layers, with asphalt on top of gravel and dirt, and we designed our anchors to work with those layers.

The hole goes all the way through the asphalt and continues into the gravel and dirt below. The adhesive then fills the hole around the anchor, creating a solid connection through the asphalt and the material beneath it.

All our anchor models can be installed in 3" asphalt using their specified drill depth and diameter. If you break through the asphalt into gravel or dirt partway down, that is normal. Keep drilling to the full depth in the [installation specifications](/installation/anchor-install/#anchor-specifications), because the anchor needs that depth to sit flush with the surface.

For example, our reference test pavement is 3" of asphalt over 3" of compacted road base. A 12" hole there passes through the 3" of asphalt and the 3" of road base, then another 6" into the ground below.

## How Thickness Affects Strength

Our reference pull testing uses 3" of asphalt over compacted road base. Asphalt thickness means the thickness of the bonded asphalt pavement, excluding the gravel or soil beneath it.

Thicker asphalt gives an anchor more material to hold onto, and field experience shows higher pull resistance in thicker pavement. We have not yet pull-tested in asphalt thicker than our 3" reference, so we cannot give a reliable thickness adjustment. In 3" asphalt the asphalt and the anchor are close to balanced; in thicker asphalt the anchor itself is more likely to become the limit, so resistance will not keep increasing in proportion to thickness.

Our [guide loads](/testing/anchor-strength-testing/#what-the-numbers-mean) assume 3" asphalt. Thicker asphalt does not establish a higher site-specific allowable load or a fixed safety margin. Pavement condition, temperature, installation, and load duration still matter. [Contact us](https://asphaltanchors.com/pages/contact) before relying on a thickness increase to meet a required application load.

We discourage using our anchors in asphalt thinner than 3".
